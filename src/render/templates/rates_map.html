Caveats and limitations

  • Three different sources, three different vintages. Relief counts and values come from the MHCLG NNDR1 supplementary workbook (England, 2026-27 forecast), StatsWales estimates (Wales, 2026-27) and the Scottish Government NDR reliefs publication (Scotland, 2025-26 snapshot). Rateable value is the comparable measure: VOA stock of properties at 31 March 2025 for England and Wales, Scottish valuation roll at 1 June 2025 for Scotland.
  • Business rates are not council tax. They are levied on non-domestic property, set nationally through a multiplier rather than locally through a band ladder, and much of the yield is pooled and redistributed rather than kept by the billing authority. The two cannot be added together.
  • Rateable value is not the bill. The bill is rateable value times the multiplier, less any relief. Rateable value measures the size of the business base, not the revenue raised from it.
  • England does not publish rateable value in NNDR1. That figure comes from a separate VOA release, which is why it carries a different date.
  • Scotland's designated utilities — 54 properties and £799m of rateable value — are valued centrally because they span council boundaries. They are excluded from this map rather than assigned to any council.
  • Two English authorities, Wigan and Woking, report no hereditament count in the source (marked not applicable).
  • Relief share compares relief value against rateable value. Because the relief and rateable value figures come from different years and different definitions, treat it as indicative rather than exact.
