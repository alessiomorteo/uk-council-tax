Caveats & limitations

  • Band D is not the same house in each nation. Bands are set against property values at 1 April 1991 in England and Scotland, but 1 April 2003 in Wales. A Band D dwelling is £68,001–£88,000 (England), £45,001–£58,000 (Scotland) and £91,001–£123,000 (Wales). Cross-nation comparisons of level are therefore indicative, not like-for-like.
  • Scotland's top bands use a different multiplier ladder. Since 1 April 2017 (SSI 2016/368) Scotland applies 473/360, 585/360, 705/360 and 882/360 to bands E–H, versus the 11/9, 13/9, 15/9, 18/9 used in England and Wales. Band H is 2.450× Band D in Scotland but 2.000× elsewhere.
  • England's per-band figures cover billing authorities only. County councils and combined authorities levy precepts but do not bill, so they are not separate rows; their precept is already inside the area total.
  • Affordability uses a household-level bill against an individual-level wage. Median gross annual pay is per job (ASHE, place of residence), while council tax is charged per dwelling and assumes at least two adults. The ratio is a relative indicator of local pressure — not what any given household actually pays after discounts, single-occupancy reductions or Council Tax Reduction.
  • Two councils have no earnings figure. City of London and Isles of Scilly are suppressed by the ONS for small sample sizes and appear grey in the affordability view.
  • Pay is 2025, council tax is 2026-27. The two inputs are one year apart; no uprating has been applied.
  • Northern Ireland is excluded. It has no band system — domestic rates are rateable value × rate poundage — so it cannot appear on a band-based map.
  • Parish precepts are included in England's area total, so two otherwise similar districts can differ because of local parish levies.
