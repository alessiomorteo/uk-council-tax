Caveats & limitations

  • Band D is not the same house in each nation. Bands are set against property values at 1 April 1991 in England and Scotland, but 1 April 2003 in Wales. A Band D dwelling is £68,001–£88,000 (England), £45,001–£58,000 (Scotland) and £91,001–£123,000 (Wales). Cross-nation comparisons of level are indicative, not like-for-like.
  • Scotland's top bands use a different multiplier ladder. Since 1 April 2017 (SSI 2016/368) Scotland applies 473/360, 585/360, 705/360 and 882/360 to bands E–H, versus 11/9, 13/9, 15/9, 18/9 in England and Wales. Band H is 2.450× Band D in Scotland but 2.000× elsewhere.
  • England's per-band figures cover billing authorities only. County councils and combined authorities levy precepts but do not bill, so they are not separate rows; their precept is already inside the area total.
  • Affordability compares a household bill with an individual wage. Median gross annual pay is per job (ASHE, place of residence), while council tax is charged per dwelling and assumes at least two adults. The ratio indicates relative local pressure, not what a given household actually pays after discounts, single-occupancy reduction or Council Tax Reduction.
  • Two councils are missing. City of London and Isles of Scilly have their earnings estimates suppressed by the ONS for small sample sizes, so they do not appear here (348 of 350 councils).
  • Pay is 2025; council tax is 2026-27. The two inputs are one year apart and no uprating has been applied.
  • Northern Ireland is excluded. It has no band system — domestic rates are rateable value × rate poundage — so it cannot appear in a band-based comparison.
  • Parish precepts are included in England's area total, so two otherwise similar districts can differ because of local parish levies.
